DeepThought — Privacy Policy

Draft — lawyer review pending before launch.
Last updated: 10 November 2026

1. Who we are and what this policy covers

1.1 DeepThought ("DeepThought", "we", "us", "our") is a moderated writing community for adults. It is an anonymous, text-only expression app where people write feelings and light stories without a public name, photo, profile, or followers.

1.2 This Privacy Policy explains what information we collect when you use DeepThought, why we collect it, where it is stored, who it is shared with, how long we keep it, and the choices and rights you have.

1.3 By creating an account or using DeepThought, you agree to this Privacy Policy. If you do not agree with it, please do not use the app.

1.4 For users in India, DeepThought's publisher is the "Data Fiduciary" for your personal data under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the "DPDP Act"): it is the publisher who decides why and how your personal data is processed. The hosting and store providers who run parts of the service for us act only on our instructions.

1.5 This policy is published in English. You may ask us to provide it in any language listed in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India, and we will do so in line with the DPDP Act.

2. The basic promise

2.1 DeepThought is built so that your real identity is never shown to other users. Other users see, at most, the signature you choose for a post (your own name, a pen-name, or the word "Anonymous") and an anonymous writer identifier that cannot be used by them to find out who you are.

2.2 Posts and other content in our database are stored with an anonymous writer identifier, not with your account identity. Your account identity stays on the server side and is never displayed publicly anywhere in the app.

2.3 There is no public profile, no follower system, and no direct messaging (DM) or chat in DeepThought. No other user can contact you, follow you, or look you up.

3. Information we collect

3.1 Account and device information

3.1.1 When you open DeepThought for the first time, an account is created for your device. You do not have to give us any name, email address, or password to begin: returning on the same device signs you back in automatically.

3.1.2 We store a protected (hashed) form of the information needed to recognise your device. We use this only to sign you back in on that device.

3.1.3 Nothing about your device account is shown to other users. There is no username and no public identity attached to it.

3.2 Optional email sign-in

3.2.1 If you choose, you can sign up with an email address and a password, so that you can sign back in on any device — for example, if you change or lose your phone. This is entirely optional; you can use DeepThought fully without ever adding one.

3.2.2 Signing up also asks for a display name. This is simply your pen-name inside the app — the name your writing can be signed with — and it is the only name that is ever shown. You can change it in your Profile. It does not have to be your real name, and we never ask for your real name.

3.2.3 To confirm that the email address is yours, sign-up sends a short verification code to that email address; the account is created when the code is entered. Your password is stored only in protected (hashed) form — we cannot read it, and we will never ask for it anywhere inside the app.

3.2.4 Your sign-in email is used for signing in, for account security (such as sign-in and deletion verification codes), and for nothing else. It is never shown to other users, never printed on your posts, and never used for marketing. Inside the app, only you see it — and even there it is shown in masked form (for example, r•••@gmail.com) in your Settings.

3.2.5 If you never add an email sign-in and you lose your device, we will have no way to verify that the account is yours, and the account cannot be restored. This is a deliberate part of the anonymous design (see the Terms of Service).

3.3 Content you create

3.3.1 We store the content you write and publish: posts (feelings, confessions, unsent letters, stories, funny posts), books and their pages, thoughts and echoes you add under other people's posts, and any drafts or private items the app saves for you, such as "Never Sent" letters. A thought is public, under the post it answers. An echo is different: it is a quiet resonance that only the author of the post it answers can see, and we store it on that basis.

3.3.2 We store the signature you attach to a post or book — the name or pen-name you choose, or "Anonymous". A signature is shown publicly because you chose to put it on that post. Your account identity behind it is not shown.

3.3.3 Content that breaks our Community Rules may be held for review by our human moderation team (the "Desk") before it appears, or kept in moderation records as described in Section 9.

3.3.4 The place on a post. If you choose to attach a place to a post, it is a coarse place — a city or an area — that you type or pick yourself. It is shown publicly with that post, because you chose to put it there. We never read your device's GPS, and we never collect your precise location. If you do not want a place shown, simply leave it off the post.

3.4 Your activity inside the app

3.4.1 We store the things the app needs to work for you personally: posts you save, writers you privately keep ("keeps"), writers you block (so their writing is no longer shown to you), your settings and preferences, your reading progress in books, and your reactions to posts. If you send us a suggestion or feedback through the in-app "Suggestion & Feedback" option, we also store the text you sent so we can read and act on it; it is not published and is not shown to other users.

3.4.2 Keeps are private. A writer is not told the name or identity of anyone who kept them, and no list of keepers is shown to anyone.

3.4.3 We do not show follower counts, like leaderboards, or any public popularity lists.

3.5 Technical logs and safety records

3.5.1 Like any online service, our systems keep technical logs (for example, the time of a request, whether it succeeded, and basic error information). We use these logs to keep the service running, to stop abuse and spam, and to enforce rate limits that protect the community.

3.5.2 We keep moderation and safety records: reports made by readers, Desk decisions, and records of accounts stopped for breaking the rules. These records protect the community and help us apply the rules fairly.

3.6 Premium and gift information

3.6.1 If you buy Premium, the payment itself is processed by Google Play Billing. We do not collect or store your card number, bank details, or UPI details. We receive from the store only what we need to know that your subscription is active, which plan it is, and when it renews or ends.

3.6.2 If you create or redeem a Gift-a-Month code, we store the code's status (created, redeemed, used, expired) linked to accounts in the protected form needed to stop a code being used twice.

4. How we use your information

4.1 We use the information described above only to:

  • (a) create, recognise, and restore your account;
  • (b) show your writing to readers and show other people's writing to you;
  • (c) run the distribution system that gives new posts a fair first audience;
  • (d) moderate content and enforce the Community Rules;
  • (e) keep your saves, keeps, settings, drafts, and reading progress available to you;
  • (f) provide Premium features and check gift codes;
  • (g) protect the service against spam, abuse, and attacks;
  • (h) answer your requests, including account export and account deletion;
  • (i) meet legal obligations that apply to us.

4.2 We do not use your writing to train artificial intelligence models. DeepThought has no AI-generated content and no bots; content in the app is written by people.

4.3 We do not sell your personal information.

5. Where your information is stored

5.1 DeepThought's data is stored on Supabase infrastructure in the Mumbai, India (ap-south-1) region.

5.2 Access to the stored data is restricted. Public-facing parts of the system can only ever read the anonymous, reader-safe version of posts; the account identity behind a post is not reachable through them.

6. Who we share information with

6.1 We share information only in the following situations:

  • (a) Service providers that run parts of DeepThought for us (for example, database hosting and the app stores). They receive only what they need to provide their part of the service, they act only on our instructions, and they are required to protect your information to the same standard set out in this policy.
  • (b) Google Play, when you buy Premium or redeem offers through the store, under Google's own terms and privacy policy.
  • (c) Legal compulsion, as described in Section 12.
  • (d) Safety, where sharing a minimal record is necessary to act on a serious rule violation or to protect a person from harm, as permitted by law.

6.2 Other users never receive your account identity, your sign-in email, your device information, or any list connecting you to the writers you keep or the posts you save.

7. Advertising

7.1 DeepThought shows no advertising at launch. The app contains no ad networks and no advertising identifiers.

7.2 If we ever introduce advertising, we will update this section first — before the advertising starts — naming the advertising partners involved, the information they receive, and the choices you will have. Any advertising will be clearly labelled as advertising inside the app.

7.3 Whether or not advertising is ever introduced, we will never sell your identity or your sign-in email to advertisers, and we will never use your writing, your saves, or your keeps to target advertisements at you or at anyone else.

8. Cookies and similar technology

8.1 The DeepThought app does not use advertising cookies. Where a web page of ours uses storage or cookies, it is only the kind needed for the page to function (for example, remembering that you completed a step in the account deletion request).

9. How long we keep information

9.1 Your posts, books, thoughts, saves, keeps, and settings are kept while your account exists, so the app can show them to you and to your readers.

9.2 When you delete your account (in the app, or through the web deletion page), your content and personal information are deleted as described in Section 11.

9.3 Moderation and safety records connected with serious rule violations may be kept for a limited period after the related content or account is removed, so that a stopped account cannot simply return and repeat the same harm. These records are not shown to other users. Where the IT Rules, 2021 require removed content and its records to be preserved for investigation, we preserve them privately for 180 days.

9.4 Technical and security logs are kept for at least 180 days and no longer than one year, for security, abuse prevention, and troubleshooting, and are then removed or anonymised.

9.5 Where the law requires us to keep a specific record for a specific period, we keep only that record, only for that period. This includes the IT Rules, 2021 requirement to preserve an account's registration record for 180 days after the account is cancelled: that minimal record is kept privately, is never shown to anyone, and is deleted when the period ends.

9.6 If a personal data breach happens, we will inform the Data Protection Board of India and each affected user without delay, as the DPDP Act, 2023 and its Rules require — telling you what happened, what it may mean for you, what we are doing about it, and what you can do to stay safe.

10. Your rights and choices

10.1 Export. You can export your account data from within the app (account export), giving you a copy of your content and account information.

10.2 Delete. You can delete your account and your data from within the app at any time. If you cannot open the app, you can request deletion through our web account-deletion page by verifying the email address you signed up with (see the "Account Deletion (Web)" document).

10.3 Optional email sign-in. Adding an email sign-in is your choice. You can add it or leave it out; leaving it out does not limit your use of the app, but it means a lost device cannot be replaced as a way back into your account.

10.4 Signature choice. For every post and book you choose: your name, a pen-name, or "Anonymous". Thoughts and echoes carry no signature.

10.5 Settings. Your in-app settings (including appearance and notification choices, where offered) are under your control in the app.

10.6 Grievance. If you believe any of your information has been handled wrongly, you may write to the Grievance Officer named in Section 14. We respond to every grievance within the period stated there.

10.7 Your rights under the DPDP Act (India). As a Data Principal you may also: ask us for a summary of the personal data we hold about you and how it is processed, and the identities of anyone we have shared it with; ask us to correct, complete, or update your personal data; and nominate another person to exercise your rights if you die or become unable to. Export (Section 10.1) and deletion (Section 10.2) are how most of these rights work inside DeepThought, because your writing is your main personal data here. For anything the app cannot do, write to the contact in Section 14.

10.8 Withdrawing consent. You may withdraw your consent to our processing at any time by deleting your account (Section 10.2) or by writing to the contact in Section 14. After withdrawal we process your data only as needed to complete the deletion and to keep the records the law requires (Section 9). If you are not satisfied with our response to a grievance, you may complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

11. Account deletion — what happens

11.1 Deleting your account is permanent. It cannot be undone.

11.2 When your account is deleted: your posts and books are removed, your thoughts and echoes are removed, your saves and keeps are removed, your settings and drafts (including "Never Sent" letters) are removed, and your sign-in email record and account identifiers, if any, are removed.

11.3 Because other users never had your identity, there is no public profile to remove; deletion removes the underlying account and its content from our systems, subject only to Sections 9.3 and 9.5.

11.4 Deleting your DeepThought account does not by itself cancel a Premium subscription bought through Google Play. Subscriptions are managed and cancelled in your Google Play account; please cancel there as well if you do not wish to be charged again.

12. Law enforcement and legal compulsion — an honest note

12.1 We protect our users' anonymity against other users and against casual requests. We do not hand over account records in response to informal requests.

12.2 However, we are not above the law, and you should know this clearly: if we receive a valid order from a court or from the police acting under law, we may be legally required to hand over the minimal record we hold for an account — the account ID, and the sign-in email address if the user provided one. Passwords are stored only in protected (hashed) form, so we hold no readable password for you, and we hold no public identity for you. We cannot hand over information we do not have.

12.3 Where the law allows it, we will limit any such disclosure to the minimum the valid order requires.

12.4 Where a court, or a lawful written direction from an authorised government officer under Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act, requires content to be removed, we remove or disable access to it within the time the IT Rules, 2021 prescribe. Where an authorised government agency lawfully orders information or assistance, we provide it within the period the Rules prescribe (72 hours).

13. Children and the 18+ rule

13.1 DeepThought is for adults only. You must be 18 years or older to use it.

13.2 We do not knowingly allow accounts of anyone under 18. If we learn that an account belongs to a person under 18, we will close that account and remove its content.

13.3 If you believe a person under 18 is using DeepThought, please report it through the app or to the Grievance Officer (Section 14).

15. Changes to this policy

15.1 If we change this Privacy Policy in an important way, we will show the change in the app before it takes effect, so you can read it and decide whether to continue using DeepThought.

15.2 The current version of this policy will always be the one published with the app and on our policy page.

DeepThought is a moderated writing community. It is not a chat service, and it is not an anonymous or random chat app.
